`sync_jobs.settle` answers inconclusive when no import ran end to end. That is
by design, so that a driver that refused at start cannot pass (OBS-GATE-006).
Two things left these worlds without a finished import.

- **No sync picked** (`36368193325` a1 and `d070`).
  - A fault-free world drives for about three seconds, and the `api` mix gives
    `sync` one pick in 17 (`api_action: 12, action: 4, sync: 1`).
  - `d070`'s six clients made 45 picks: P(no sync in 45) = (16/17)^45 ≈ 6.5%.
    `36368193325` a1 made 51 picks (87 operations): 4.5%.
  - That alone cannot explain 7 of 32. P(at least 7 of 32) is about 0.05–0.4%
    at 4.5–6.5% an attempt.
- **Syncs that did not finish** (`36366359928` a1, and `36345175707` a1).
  - In the master run, the driver ended `done: ok=56 fail=0 info=2`, and
    worker-a's log shows `job 1 (mapping.sync) attempt 2 of 3: Failed query:
    insert into audit_log …`. The import's first attempt failed at its audit
    entry.
  - `36345175707` a1 printed its driver, app and worker logs and shows the same:
    `sync [1 4 5 6]` runnable, `done: ok=51 fail=0 info=2`, and the same failed
    `audit_log` insert.
  - The database's error is not in those log tails. It is likely OBS-GATE-001's
    finding 2, the audit writer's deadlock, which OBS-GATE-005 (finding 3)
    lists as open.
  - Since #11, CI keeps each run's bundle. The kept bundles show the deadlock
    itself: `ER_LOCK_DEADLOCK` on the audit append in the app log, behind HTTP
    500s (10 and 14 in two runs of the signed-in-clients world). A fix is
    proposed in #14.

The signed-in-clients world passed on `36344923479`'s a2 re-run. `2afdbb7`
(`36343523222`) was not re-run. The branch's next commit, `0ca4351`, which

a database that is still recovering, or boot again. On a shared CI runner that
can take longer than the window the tool allows after HEAL. Whether the app
could come back faster is a separate question from whether the window fits CI.

## 3. Also seen

Every CI verdict carries `Checker: … the program(s) that decided this verdict
changed since the lock was written`. It is a warning, not a failure, and no
oracle's source has changed since `820937d`.
- The lock records the oracle programs' digests as built on the maintainer's
  host (`bin/oracle-*.exe`).
- CI's digests differ from those, and from each other on every commit. For
  example, oracle-sync-jobs was `c555a05b91ea` on `a4e900a`, `495e7d2c2f38` on
  `a6123b5`, and `bcc3c56653d9` on `468918c`'s run. They match only across
  re-runs of one commit.
- `build-tools.ps1` builds with `go build -trimpath` but without
  `-buildvcs=false`, so each binary probably embeds its commit.
- Re-locking with CI's digests would therefore quiet the warning for one commit
  only. A lasting fix needs reproducible builds first, which is a change to the
  build script.

## For a decision

These touch pre-registered profiles, the harness, an oracle or the lock, so
none is made here:

1. **API world vacuous.** For the syncs that did not finish, the audit writer's
   deadlock is a defect in Ontos, not in the world (#14). For the syncs never
   picked, the options are:
   - raise the `sync` weight in the mixed profiles, e.g. `api: {api_action: 12, action: 4, sync: 4}`;
   - have the driver start each such world with one import followed to the end;
   - lengthen the fault-free drive.

   Each needs a pre-registration addendum. Leaving `sync_jobs.settle` as it is
   keeps OBS-GATE-006's guard.
2. **Liveness window on CI.** Measure how long the app takes to come back on a
   CI runner after each fault. Then decide between a wider window for CI, a
   faster recovery in the app, or both.
3. **The digest warning.** Make the checker builds reproducible, then re-lock
   with a reason. Or leave the warning.

The runs above were re-run. How such a run is to be read is left to the
maintainer.
